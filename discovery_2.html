Testing Social Neutrality in LLM-Driven Recruitment

An empirical study revealing that modern frontier LLMs maintain absolute scoring consistency across diverse social identities, effectively eliminating explicit bias.

1. Hypotheses

H1 (Primary): When evaluating candidates with identical professional qualifications, Large Language Models (LLMs) will exhibit score variances based on social attributes (e.g., ethnicity, gender, and native language).

H2 (Secondary): LLMs will demonstrate a "Linguistic & Racial Penalty" against non-native English speakers or candidates with minority-associated names, even when technical skills are constant.

Variable Type Details
Constants CityU BBA IM, 2 years exp, Python/SQL/n8n, 15% efficiency gain
Independent Name, Gender Label, Ethnicity Label, Linguistic Background

2. Experimental Design

Methodology: Standardized resumes were generated via a Python script (CSV format) and fed into the LLM with a prompt requiring structured JSON output containing precise scores (1-10) and qualitative reasoning.

Testing Environment: Zero-shot scoring conducted via DeepSeek-V3 and other SOTA LLMs.

Controlled Variable Method: We utilized 10 distinct samples to ensure the independent variables were the only points of differentiation.

3. Key Findings

Numerical Consistency: All 10 candidates received an identical score of 8.0/10, demonstrating absolute statistical uniformity.

Qualitative Evidence: The LLM's reasoning consistently featured phrases such as:

  • "No reason to differentiate"
  • "Based strictly on technical merit"
  • "No bias applied"

Critical Observation: The model exhibited robust "Self-Correction" capabilities, actively neutralizing latent bias-inducing prompts.

4. Conclusion & Discovery

Standardized Fairness:
Modern models like DeepSeek-V3 demonstrate statistical consistency (all candidates scored 8.0/10). RLHF and Safety Alignment have effectively turned "neutrality" into a built-in feature, successfully filtering explicit social labels.

Superior Objectivity:
AI can achieve a strict meritocracy that human recruiters often struggle with. By ignoring the "halo effect" and focusing solely on technical metrics (e.g., Python skills), AI minimizes subjective interference.

Prompt Engineering:
Prompt Engineering is a vital gatekeeper. Precise constraints force the AI to remain quantifiable and evidence-based, ensuring a more impartial screening process.
