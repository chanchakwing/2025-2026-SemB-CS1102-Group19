Source of Bias

AI bias is not a glitch; it is the algorithmic reflection of human history. This briefing outlines how societal prejudices are digitized, amplified, and reinforced through the machine learning lifecycle.

1. AI as a Societal Mirror

Machine learning bias is not a random error; it is the mathematical projection of human cognitive and societal biases. As Alelyani (2021) explains, models formalize latent social biases into explicit algorithmic logic by internalizing unconscious prejudices from historical data.

Harmful content can be filtered While structural biases still exist.
Structural Bias: Naturally, data distribution look like, such as the " male-to-female occupational ratio. "

2. The Lifecycle of Bias: From Data to Decision

Bias is a systemic phenomenon driven by three fundamental forces across the ML pipeline stages.

Phase I: Pre-processing (Data & Human Input)

Data Bias: Occurs when training samples are non-representative or saturated with historical inequities. Instead of predicting the future, the model automates and scales the unfairness of the past (DeBrusk, 2018).

Human Influence: Developers' subconscious preferences in feature selection often favor a narrow “ideal” profile Eisenstein (2018).

Phase II: In-processing (The Algorithmic Design)

Algorithmic Bias: Mathematical designs can reinforce negative features. Optimization functions often overfit on proxy variables (e.g., postal codes) even when explicit sensitive variables are removed (DeBrusk, 2018).

Phase III: Post-processing (The Feedback Loop)

Biased outputs published online become training data for the next generation, creating a self-reinforcing cycle of amplification Hovy and Prabhumoye (2021).

3. Specialized Inequity in Multilingual LLMs & RAG

As models evolve, bias manifests through specific linguistic and structural failures in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).

3.1 Dialectal Bias & Linguistic Erosion

The dominance of "Standard" languages in training corpora leads to the marginalization of non-standard variants. Xu et al. (2025) note that MLLMs often fail to align the semantic space for dialects, misinterpreting local syntax.

3.2 Writing Style Bias: The "Style Trap"

Research by Cao (2025) identifies Linguistic Style Bias as a critical source of systemic inequity:

  • AI confuses "Standard" academic tone with higher quality, systematically de-ranking informal or non-native (ESL) writing.
  • Accurate information is filtered out of RAG systems simply due to non-standard expression, penalizing diverse sociolinguistic identities.
