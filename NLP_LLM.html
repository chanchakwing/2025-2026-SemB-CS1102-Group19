Large Language Model (LLM)

Refer to the Research Team, Wang et al. (2024) state that The current era of models like GPT-4 and Llama 3, characterized by billions of parameters. The development is from small to large foundation model.

Mechanism: models exhibit Emergent Abilities. They don't just predict the next word; they follow instructions and perform complex reasoning across almost all human knowledge.

The Challenge: models grow, they become "Black Boxes."

Social Equity Insight: are now Implicit and Brittle. While LLMs are trained to be "explicitly unbiased," they often harbor hidden prejudices (Implicit Bias) that only surface in subtle decision-making tasks, such as hiring or legal drafting support.

Exploring the Core of LLMs:

Refer to the Research Team, Vaswani et al., (2017) proposed the Transformer Architecture.
The Transformer model acts as a "digital version" of the human brain, converting external messages into signals that can be processed and understood by machines.

  • Message Translation:
  • Message Learning:
  • QKV Matching:
  • Reply Generation:

1. Message Translation




  • Tokenization: Raw text is decomposed into smaller units called "tokens." Each token is assigned a unique numerical ID..



  • Embedding: Based on these IDs, the system looks up corresponding vectors from a high-dimensional "Vector Dictionary.".



  • Positional Encoding: This step injects information about the position of words in a sequence. In language, word order is fundamental to understanding semantic meaning.

2. Message Learning

The human brain understands meaning through Context. A "bat" can be an animal or sports equipment depending on the surrounding words.

Self-Attention Mechanism:

The Transformer replicates biological selective attention, allowing the model to focus on relevant words within a sentence to resolve ambiguity.

3. The Waltz of Self-Attention: Q, K, and V

In this process, every token produces three distinct vectors:

  • Query (Q): "What am I looking for?"
  • Key (K): "What do I have to offer?"
  • Value (V): "The actual information I provide."

Query-Key Matching: Scores are calculated to determine how much "attention" one word should pay to another.

4. Reply Generation: Iterative Decoding

Unlike humans who may think of an entire sentence at once, LLMs generate responses through a word-by-word (token-by-token) cyclic process.

Core Mechanism: Predict the Next Token

At its essence, an LLM is a powerful "probability prediction engine." To generate a response, it performs the following steps:

  • Input Context: The model receives the original prompt plus any text already generated.
  • Probability Distribution: Through calculations across Transformer layers, the model assigns a probability score to every token in its vocabulary.
  • Selection (Sampling): The model selects the most appropriate token based on these probabilities. (e.g., Given "AI is", the model predicts "future" has the highest probability (0.8) and selects it).
  • Iterative Loop: The newly generated token is fed back into the input, and the process repeats until an End-of-Sequence token is generated.
