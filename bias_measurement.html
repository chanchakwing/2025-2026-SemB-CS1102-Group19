Bias Measurement

This webpage explores methods for measuring algorithmic bias, covering quantitative metrics, fairness principles, and the "benchmark trap" in multilingual LLMs.

1. How is Bias Measured?

Measuring bias requires moving from subjective observation to quantitative auditing. The following methods are commonly employed to detect systemic inequities:

  • Score Deviation: Creating template sentences that vary only by a protected attribute (e.g., "He is a doctor" vs. "She is a doctor") and comparing the model's sentiment scores.
  • Individual Fairness: Evaluating if the model assigns identical sentiment predictions to individuals who differ only by attributes like name or gender.
  • Group Fairness: Examining whether sentiment distributions differ significantly across demographic, racial, or ethnic groups.
  • Quantitative Metrics: Using established tools like Average Odds Difference and Disparate Impact to compute concrete bias scores.

2. Metrics for Evaluating Algorithmic Fairness

To move toward technical auditing, Alelyani (2021) outlines three essential statistical metrics:

  1. Statistical Parity: Ensures that different demographic groups receive positive outcomes (e.g., being hired) at an equal rate, independent of protected attributes.
  2. Equal Opportunity: Focuses on the True Positive Rate. It ensures that qualified individuals have an equal probability of being correctly identified, regardless of their group background.
  3. Predictive Parity: Examines whether a model’s predictive scores (e.g., a 70% success probability) carry the same mathematical meaning and accuracy across all populations.

3. Evaluation Bias and the "Benchmark Trap"

Most metrics used to evaluate Multilingual LLM (MLLM) performance are translated from English-centric benchmarks, leading to Evaluation Bias. These "Benchmark Traps" create a false impression of proficiency while masking failures in understanding native dialect logic.

According to Xu et al. (2025), there is an urgent need for "Native-First" evaluation sets curated by mother-tongue speakers to measure true linguistic fairness.
