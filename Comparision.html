Comparing Traditional vs. LLM-based Systems

To understand the evolution of social equity in computing, we compare Traditional Statistical Methods with Modern Large Language Models.

A. Information Retrieval Area

According to the randomized experiment by Spatharioti et al. (2023), the shift to LLM-based tools significantly impacts how information is retrieved and perceived.

Performance Metric Traditional Search LLM-based Search
Average Queries per Task Higher Significantly Fewer
Query Type Keyword Fragments Complex Natural Language
Completion Time Baseline (Slower) Significantly Faster
User Reliance Skeptical / Independent verification Overreliance (High trust in direct answers)

The more "user-friendly" technology becomes, the more "vulnerable" humanity's defenses against bias actually become.

1. A Qualitative Change in Query Behavior:

In Traditional Search (Keyword Fragments):
Users are like "placing an order," entering "Hong Kong housing policy impact."
This forces the brain to maintain analytical thinking, expecting to see multiple links.

LLM Search (Natural Language):
Users are like "having a conversation," asking, "How will this policy affect me?".
This conversational interaction induces the brain to enter a "trust mode," viewing AI as a knowledgeable advisor rather than a cold, impersonal database.

2. The Synthesis Trap:

Information Gatekeeper:
In the traditional model, the search engine is like a "librarian,"
Guiding you to different bookshelves.
In the LLM model, AI is the "sole author," directly reading the book and writing a summary for you.

Invisible Eradication:
If AI weighs information based on "frequency of occurrence" during synthesis, then the voices of minority groups (such as non-mainstream dialects, subcultural viewpoints, and non-mainstream academic claims) will be filtered out as "noise."

3. The Quantitative Threat of Overreliance:

Research indicates that while LLM increases speed, users' sensitivity to misinformation decreases.
In a context of social equity, If AI provides career advice with gender or racial bias, users are highly likely to adopt it directly due to its authoritative tone and fluent synthesis.

B. Semantic Analysis Area

The "Paradigm Shift" of Bias:

Based on the research by Chuthamsatid et al. (2025)
AI model biases have not vanished with technological progress.
Instead, they have evolved into deeper, structural issues that are harder to detect.
For example, In the past, we could easily detect AI making discriminatory remarks; now AI has learned to be "polite," but its underlying logic still contains a preference for specific ethnic groups.
Current technical evolution presents a dangerous trend: superficial fairness (Data Beautification) often masks underlying structural injustice.
Developers manually intervene to make the output appear correct, but this only addresses the downstream issue and does not resolve the uneven distribution of data upstream.

Modern language models exhibit bias patterns fundamentally different from traditional models:

Table 1: Bias Dimension Comparison
Bias Dimension Statistical Methods (GloVe) Modern LLMs (OpenAI/Google)
Gender Alignment Strong Male-skewed associations Shifted (~60%+ Female-alignment)
Racial Bias (SC-WEAT) Co-occurrence based stereotypes Structural Underrepresentation (e.g., Black-associated words)
Thematic Clustering Static and explicit Dynamic and hidden within high-dimensional space
  • 1. Gender Alignment Shift: "Over-correction"
    • Phenomenon & Data: Modern models (e.g., OpenAI) show over 60% Female-alignment.
    • Technical Essence: This is the result of human intervention (such as RLHF/Fine-tuning) aimed at eliminating explicit biases like "male doctor/female nurse." Developers injected massive amounts of alignment data for this purpose.
    • Fairness Risks: This "Data Beautification" may trigger new imbalances. If over-corrected for political correctness, the model may lose accuracy when processing real-world data involving biological differences (e.g., medical advice).


    • Reason:To avoid stereotypes like "male doctor/female nurse," engineers fed the model a large amount of data with "progressive value."
    As a result, the model subconsciously over-biases towards women in neutral contexts in order to "safety."

  • 2. Racial Bias: From "Stereotypes" to "Structural Underrepresentation"
    • Comparison: In traditional models , bias is explicit and measurable .
      In modern Large Language Models (LLMs), bias has turned implicit.
      • Previously, we could use mathematical formulas to directly calculate how close "Black names" and "negative words" were.
    • Structural Underrepresentation:
      Models no longer directly produce discriminatory associations.
      Instead, the culture, dialects, or contexts of specific groups (e.g., Black-associated terms) are marginalized within the vector space.
      • AI doesn't "hate" anyone; it simply consider the minority as the "noise" in vector space.

C. From small model to Large Model

Table 2: Technical Evaluation: Transparency vs. High-Dimensional Black Box
Evaluation Feature Traditional Methods (GloVe/FastText) Modern LLMs (Transformer-based)
Dimension & Logic Low-dimensional (300D), relationships are linear. Extremely high-dimensional (thousands of D), relationships are non-linear and dynamic.
Interpretability High. Distances can be directly calculated via cosine similarity. Low. Bias is hidden within multi-layer attention mechanisms.
Bias Manifestation Static Stereotypes. Dynamic Structural Bias (Hidden Structural Bias).

Traditional models are like a straight line, where you can easily find the bias points; modern models are like a giant maze, with biases distributed in countless tiny details across thousands of dimensions.

Social Fairness Warning: The Injustice Hard to Audit

Technological progress has effectively "raised the threshold for discrimination," making unjust behavior harder to monitor and prosecute:

  • Audit Difficulty Skyrockets: In the past, simple scripts could prove bias. Now, bias is hidden in minute perturbations of high-dimensional vectors, rendering traditional auditing tools ineffective.
  • Misleading Data Beautification: Companies may use impressive "gender alignment rates" to claim model fairness while ignoring the fact that minority semantics are being "marginalized."
  • Invisible Erasure Effect: As AI becomes social infrastructure for hiring and loans, this "hidden injustice" causes marginalized groups to be excluded unknowingly. Because system answers appear "objective," victims find it difficult to even raise charges.

D. Core Conclusion

Technological progress has not eradicated injustice; it has made it "deeper" and "more concealed."
While pursuing LLM efficiency, it is necessary to develop fairness detection tools capable of diving into high-dimensional spaces to prevent AI from becoming an invisible machine that erases diversity.
