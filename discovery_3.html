LLM Application: Constructing an "Ethical Layer" via Prompt Engineering

After Studying the paper including (Pawlik, 2025) and (Bura et al., 2025) Try to utilizing structured Prompt Engineering techniques to establish a technical "Ethical Layer" at the application level. This layer automatically filters model biases and ensures social equity in all decision-making outputs.

1. Key Technical Layers: Bias Mitigation Strategies

In production environments, developers can flexibly deploy three primary prompting techniques to fortify system fairness:

Technique Core Advantage Illustrative Example
Adversarial Highest efficacy; forces self-audit. "Review your previous response for any hidden gender bias and provide a neutral revision."
Role-based (DE&I) Most practical for system prompts. "Act as a DE&I consultant to ensure this recruitment ad uses inclusive language for all cultures."
Chain-of-Thought Highest transparency; traces rationale. "List your evaluation criteria step-by-step and explain how each ensures fairness before deciding."

2. Adoption in Hong Kong context

Semantic Equity for Mixed-code: Developing prompts to accurately parse "Chinglish" or Cantonese-English mixtures. This ensures AI identifies professional merit without penalizing candidates for non-standard linguistic habits common in Hong Kong.

Hong Kong Proprietary AI Prompts: A strategic asset involving industry terminology alignment (e.g., differentiating Casual/Freelance vs. Contract) and local qualification weighting (DSE/HKCEE).

3. Application

Here are the three techniques for addressing bias and social equity, specifically tailored for the Hong Kong professional context:

1. Adversarial Prompting


Prompt:
"The Executive Assistant (EA) job description you drafted is problematic. Please act as an EOC (Equal Opportunities Commission) Legal Consultant. Identify any unfair biases like 'pleasant appearance' or 'young and energetic' that imply gender or age discrimination. Rewrite the wording to be strictly professional and gender-neutral."

Effect:
It prevents the "EA" role from being stereotyped as a position only for young women, shifting the focus to "Professional demeanor and communication skills."

2. Role-based Prompting


Prompt:
"You are a Progressive HK HR Director who champions family-friendly workplaces. Write an internal email encouraging male staff to take their full Paternity Leave. Your stance is that caregiving is a shared responsibility, not just a 'woman's job.' Ensure the tone is supportive and avoids the 'traditional breadwinner' stereotype."

Effect:
It fosters a modern corporate culture in HK, explicitly stating that taking leave will not negatively impact one's Appraisal.

3. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting


Prompt:
"I have candidates from 'Big 3' (HKU, CUHK, UST) and non-prestigious schools.
Before recommending, think step-by-step:
1. List the core Technical Skills required (e.g., n8n, Python).
2. Analyze if 'University Brand' actually correlates with these specific tasks.
3. Check if you are ignoring practical project experience due to the 'Brand Name Effect' common in HK society."

Effect:
It breaks the local "prestige bias," ensuring that candidates with hands-on technical experience (like Supabase or RAG) are not overlooked for a famous school name.

4. Conclusion and Discovery: Application-First Governance

  • Technology as Mechanism: Prompt engineering is a technical defense for Social Equity, not just performance.
  • Workflow Impact: While models have latent biases, the application workflow determines the final social consequence.
