Bias Mitigation

Mitigation involves technical data augmentation, model-tier fairness constraints, governance auditing, and inference-time solutions like prompt engineering and multi-agent frameworks.

1. Fundamental Technical Interventions

Identifying bias is only the first step; technical intervention is required to ensure equity. According to Zhao et al. (2019), two primary methods effectively reduce systemic bias:

  • Data Augmentation (CDA): Balancing training sets by swapping gendered terms (e.g., changing "he" to "she"). This prevents the model from associating specific roles with a single gender.
  • Neutralization (Debiasing): A mathematical intervention in Word Embedding space to remove "gender directions" from neutral words like "doctor" or "manager" without full retraining.

2. A Three-Tier Strategy for Social Equity

To move beyond simple fixes, Ho et al. (2025) propose a comprehensive framework:

  1. Data Tier: Implementing strategic augmentation to ensure equitable representation across all contexts.
  2. Model Tier: Integrating Fairness Constraints into loss functions to minimize reliance on sensitive demographic variables.
  3. Governance Tier: Establishing rigorous Algorithmic Auditing and human-machine collaboration to hold systems accountable (Kodiyan, 2019).

3. Low-Cost Inference-Time Solutions

Beyond retraining, strategic interaction can significantly lower bias. Research by Ling et al. (2025) explores Prompt Engineering:

  • Chain-of-Thought (CoT): Encouraging "fairness logic" step-by-step processing.
  • Positive Role-playing: Instructing the model to adopt an ethical persona to reduce stereotypical outputs.
  • System Prompt Intervention: Declaring explicit egalitarian beliefs (e.g., "I treat all races equally") acts as a powerful baseline defense (Bai et al., 2024).

4. MOMA: Multi-Agent Framework for Mitigation

Research by Xu et al. (2025) introduces MOMA, which solves the trade-off between Fairness and Utility:

  • Bias Identification Agent: Scans inputs for sensitive triggers.
  • Causal Intervention Agent: Breaks the "shortcut connection" between identity markers and stereotypes using causal inference.
  • Answer Fusion Agent: Ensures the final output is both factually accurate and socially equitable.
